# Supplementary Table S2. Multiscale Microfluidic Fabrication Strategies

Representative strategies for combining micro-scale features with millimeter-scale wells or tall structures are summarized below. The table is intended to contextualize the trade-offs motivating a plate-format, no-punch PDMS workflow.

| Approach | Micro-scale features | Macro wells / tall structures | Plate / automation compatibility | Typical trade-off | Representative refs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional PDMS soft lithography + manual punching | SU-8 photolithography for micron-scale channels | Biopsy punch after casting | Limited by manual post-processing; punching becomes the throughput bottleneck | Manual labor, variability, edge defects, difficult to scale to 96-well format | Baseline approach; see Taylor et al., 2005 |
| CO2 laser micromachining of PDMS | Maskless laser ablation; practical widths generally >100 um | Laser-ablated reservoirs, wells, or channels | Serial process; not ideal for dense plate arrays | Limited resolution, non-ideal cross-sections, thermal artifacts | Holle et al., 2007 |
| Micro-macro hybrid master via milling + hot embossing | Hot embossing for microfeatures | PMMA milled macrostructures | Can replicate PDMS masters in quantity, but not inherently plate-format | Requires hot embosser and milling; multi-master workflow | Park et al., 2010 |
| Thick SU-8 molding + photolithography | Photolithography on molded SU-8 surface | Millimeter structures molded from 3D-printed master via PDMS mold | Demonstrated as micro-to-mm seamless structures, but not as plate workflows | Surface roughness can degrade lithography; multi-step SU-8 handling | Tamura and Suzuki, 2019 |
| Direct 3D printing of PDMS onto photolithographic master | Photolithographic master for microchannels | Printed PDMS walls or compartments | Removes punching for certain architectures; not inherently microtiter plate-format | Requires PDMS-printing hardware and ink tuning | Kajtez et al., 2020 |
| Aligned SLA printing onto patterned chips | SU-8 lithography on diced chips | SLA-printed micro/macro 3D features | Chip-scale alignment; not wafer-scale or plate-scale workflows | Requires dicing and alignment fixtures; first-layer overcure effects | Pan et al., 2022 |
| 3D-printed inserts bonded to SU-8 | SU-8 photolithography for microfeatures | Tall 3D-printed inserts + epoxy bonding | Works for open reservoirs; scaling limited by manual insert handling | Metal printing cost/availability; manual placement effort | Ristola et al., 2019 |
| Hard-plastic microtiter microfluidic plates | Hot-embossed microchannels in COC | CNC-milled wells/compartments + fusion bonding | ANSI/SLAS compatible and automation-friendly | Requires specialized tooling/processes; not typical academic fabrication | Moll et al., 2024 |
| Commercial microfluidic titer plates | Proprietary | Proprietary | High-throughput, plate footprint | Limited design freedom, cost, opaque fabrication details | Spijkers et al., 2021 |
| This work: wafer-bonded resin well inserts + SU-8 microfeatures | SU-8/SUEX photolithography for micron-scale microfeatures | Resin-printed well inserts adhesively bonded to wafer mold; wells formed during casting | ANSI/SLAS footprint; demonstrated with HCS imaging and autofocus | Requires reliable bonding workflow and final mold insulation | This work |
