# Supplementary Table S1. Multiscale microfluidic fabrication strategies

Representative strategies for combining micrometer-scale features with millimeter-scale wells or other tall structures are summarized below. The comparison emphasizes the operations and equipment each strategy adds to conventional PDMS soft lithography.

| Approach and references | Features and well formation | Format and production mode | Added workflow and equipment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional PDMS soft lithography with manual punching (Baseline approach; see Taylor et al., 2005) | Microfeatures: SU-8 photolithography for micrometer-scale channels. Wells/tall structures: Openings made with a biopsy punch after casting. | Each opening is made separately after casting | Biopsy punching and manual debris removal during every device-production cycle |
| CO2 laser micromachining of PDMS (Holle et al., 2007) | Microfeatures: Maskless laser-ablated trenches whose profile depends on power, pulse density, and traverse speed. Wells/tall structures: Laser-ablated PDMS channels rather than cast well features. | Computer-controlled but serial processing of PDMS sheets | CO2 laser processing during each device-production cycle |
| Hybrid micro- and millimeter-scale mold via milling and hot embossing (Park et al., 2010) | Microfeatures: Hot embossing for microfeatures. Wells/tall structures: PMMA-milled large structures. | Batch device production from a reusable PDMS mold | Milling, hot embossing, and a workflow involving several molds |
| Thick SU-8 molding and photolithography (Tamura and Suzuki, 2019) | Microfeatures: Photolithography on a molded SU-8 surface. Wells/tall structures: Millimeter structures molded from a 3D-printed pattern through an intermediate PDMS mold. | Continuous micro- and millimeter-scale structures | Printed-pattern preparation, intermediate molding, and multi-step SU-8 handling |
| Direct 3D printing of PDMS onto a photolithographic mold (Kajtez et al., 2020) | Microfeatures: Photolithographic mold for microchannels. Wells/tall structures: Printed PDMS walls or compartments. | Automated deposition of selected large structures | PDMS-printing hardware, material formulation, and print control during production |
| Resin-printed molds and parts retained in the finished device (Meynard et al., 2025) | Microfeatures: Printed molds form the PDMS microstructures. Wells/tall structures: A separate printed cap defines and closes the culture chambers. | Eight parallel chambers aligned to a 96-well plate grid for robotic and microscope compatibility | Printer and resin selection, post-treatment, mixed-material assembly, and perfusion hardware |
| Aligned resin printing onto patterned chips (Pan et al., 2022) | Microfeatures: SU-8 lithography on diced chips. Wells/tall structures: Resin-printed micro- and millimeter-scale 3D features. | Aligned structures on individual chips | Chip dicing, resin-printing equipment, and chip-level alignment fixtures |
| 3D-printed inserts bonded to SU-8 (Ristola et al., 2019) | Microfeatures: SU-8 photolithography for micrometer-scale features. Wells/tall structures: Tall 3D-printed metal inserts bonded with epoxy. | Open cell compartments at individual-device scale | Metal 3D printing and individual alignment and bonding of three inserts on each mold |
| Hard-plastic microtiter microfluidic plates (Moll et al., 2024) | Microfeatures: Hot-embossed microchannels in COC. Wells/tall structures: CNC-milled wells or compartments with fusion bonding. | ANSI/SLAS-compatible automated plate format | CNC milling, hot embossing, layer alignment, and fusion bonding |
| Commercial microfluidic titer plates (Spijkers et al., 2021) | Microfeatures: Proprietary phaseguide-based microfluidics. Wells/tall structures: Proprietary. | 384-well plate footprint containing 40 tissue-culture chips | Commercial procurement and vendor-defined geometries |
| This work: wafer-bonded resin well inserts with SU-8/SUEX features | Microfeatures: SU-8/SUEX photolithography for micrometer-scale features. Wells/tall structures: An aligned resin insert array is bonded once to the reusable mold; all wells form during casting. | Standard plate dimensions and 9 mm well grid; demonstrated with high-content imaging and autofocus | Resin-array printing, one-time alignment and bonding, and parylene coating of the reusable mold; repeated device production uses PDMS casting and glass bonding |
